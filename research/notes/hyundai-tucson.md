# Hyundai Tucson: research notes

Family file: `data/families/hyundai-tucson.json`. Generations: JM (2004), LM (2009; ix35 / Tucson ix), TL (2015, plus the 2018 facelift), NX4 (2020, plus the 2023 facelift, SWB and LWB) and NX5 (revealed in August 2026).

## Sources used
- English Wikipedia, "Hyundai Tucson", oldid 1379303360 (secondary). This is the main source for JM and LM, and for most of the codes, dates, plants and engine tables.
- Hyundai Motor UK newsroom, "The all-new Hyundai Tucson" press kit (23 Dec 2020). Gives the NX4 SWB dimensions, luggage figures and UK/EU powertrains.
- Hyundai Motor UK newsroom, Tucson Plug-in Hybrid release (10 Dec 2020).
- Hyundai Motor UK newsroom, 2018 New York facelift release (28 Mar 2018). Gives the TL facelift changes and the European engines.
- Hyundai Motor Europe newsroom, "New Tucson with more refined and powerful design" (21 Nov 2023). Covers the NX4 facelift.
- Hyundai Motor Europe newsroom, all-new TUCSON release (1 Oct 2026). Gives the NX5 European technical specifications.

## Sources that failed
- hyundainews.com (US): release pages (/releases/3189, 3267, 3275, 4931) are a Nuxt app rendered by JavaScript, so text.mjs gets no text. The spec PDF also gives no text. As a result there is no US manufacturer data, and the US/LWB figures come from Wikipedia.
- The hyundai.news Tucson technical-data PDF (Dec 2019) is compressed, so it gives no text.
- The hyundai.news "two decades" story page body is not in the HTML.
- German Wikipedia permalinks for the per-generation articles timed out because of rate limiting. Only `Hyundai ix35` (oldid 271071012) resolved, and it was not used.

## Market distinctions
- LM: sold as ix35 in Europe, Australia and China. In Korea it was "Tucson ix", and in North America it stayed "Tucson". From TL onwards the name is Tucson globally. In China the NX4 is "Tucson L", and the LWB NX4 in Taiwan is also "Tucson L".
- NX4 comes in two wheelbases:
  - SWB (2,680 mm) is built in Nošovice for Europe, the Middle East and Mexico.
  - LWB (2,755 mm) goes to North America, China and most other markets.
  - The dimension records are kept separate: d1 is the UK/EU SWB from Hyundai, and d2 is the LWB wheelbase only, from Wikipedia. Wikipedia gives LWB length only as a range (4,630–4,670 mm).
- NX5: a standard wheelbase car for Europe (2,720 mm, Hyundai EU) and a LWB car (2,785 mm, Wikipedia) for NA, KR, AU and CN.

## Conflicts and caveats
- TL facelift 1.6 diesel: Hyundai gives 115/133 PS, while the Wikipedia table gives 115/136 PS. The Hyundai figure is recorded.
- JM: Wikipedia's production years are 2004–2009, but the brief said 2004–2010. Local assembly continued in Taiwan and Ukraine to 2010 and in Brazil to 2019.
- Most Wikipedia engine-table rows carry no market, so they are recorded as `unstated`.
- The LM 465 L luggage figure is taken from the TL section's "from 465 to 513 litres".
- The UK press kit gives luggage figures without naming a standard, so they are recorded as "not stated".

## Gaps
- No manufacturer source for JM or LM.
- There are no seating sources, except 5 seats for the NX5 EU car.
- LM and NX4 facelift reveal dates are not quotable. LM has the Korean on-sale date of 2 May 2013. NX4 has Wikipedia's "December 2023" release with no market; the Hyundai EU release is dated 21.11.2023, but the date is not in a quotable sentence.
- NX5 production start is not yet sourced; Wikipedia says "to commence" in 2026.
- No Commons category exists for the TL facelift. The checked categories are JM, LM, TL, NX4 (+SWB/LWB) and NX5; the coordinator added these.
- The 2019 China-only TL facelifts and the China-only ix35 (NU) are not recorded.
